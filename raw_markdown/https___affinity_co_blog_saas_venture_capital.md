Software-as-a-service (SaaS) has emerged as one of the most profitable and prominent investment categories in venture capital (VC). In 2024, VC investment in global enterprise software companies [totaled](https://app.dealroom.co/curated-heatmaps/funding/industry/f/growth_stages/not_mature/is_verified/anyof_yes/rounds/not_GRANT_SPAC%20PRIVATE%20PLACEMENT/tags/not_outside%20tech?endYear=2024&heatMapType=industry&id%5B0%5D=70553&id%5B1%5D=78935&interval=yearly&rows=gaming%7Ehealth%7Etravel%7Elegal%7Esecurity%7Efintech%7Ewellness%2520beauty%7Emusic%7Ereal%2520estate%7Efashion%7Esports%7Efood%7Emedia%7Edating%7Etelecom%7Eeducation%7Eenergy%7Ekids%7Ehosting%7Ehome%2520living%7Eevent%2520tech%7Erobotics%7Ejobs%2520recruitment%7Etransportation%7Esemiconductors%7Emarketing%7Eenterprise%2520software&sort=-_2023&startYear=2017&type=amount&id=80079) $92.6 billion—a 65% year-over-year increase, primarily driven by higher activity levels in Q4.

The business models of SaaS startups have proven highly scalable, which has made them a top target for both angel investors and venture funds seeking high returns.

In this article, we'll explore the top seven VC firms investing in SaaS across stages—from pre-seed and early-stage investments to growth-stage and late-stage investments—based on [Dealroom’s SaaS investor ranking](https://dealroom.co/guides/saas), which ranks VC firms by the number of B2B SaaS deals they have invested in over the last five years. We’ll also take a look at what differentiates leading VC firms.

## Key takeaways

- Global venture capital investment in SaaS companies reached $92.6 billion in 2024, a 65% year-over-year increase.
- There is diversity in the SaaS investment landscape, with leading VC firms differentiating themselves through both investment stage and approach; some focus on early-stage investments (like Antler) while others prefer Series A and B rounds (like Tiger Global and Insight Partners).
- Top-performing venture capital firms share three key characteristics: they maintain more frequent communication, focus on deepening existing relationships rather than expanding their networks, and analyze multiple data sources for deal evaluation.


## 1\. [Tiger Global Management](https://www.tigerglobal.com/)

Tiger Global Management is a New York-based investment firm that invests in public and private companies in the internet, software, cybersecurity, consumer, and fintech sectors. With a reputation for backing some of the world's most innovative companies, Tiger Global Management has an impressive track record of [90+ portfolio company IPOs](https://www.linkedin.com/company/tiger-global-management/about/) and [150 exits](https://www.crunchbase.com/organization/tiger-global/recent_investments)—including SaaS names such as Palantir and Procore. Their [typical first round](https://dealroom.co/guides/saas) is Series B.

When it comes to their investment ethos, Tiger Global Management [says](https://linkedin.com/company/tiger-global-management/about/),

> “With a strong track record investing across sectors and stages, Tiger Global aims to be a thought partner to companies and their management teams across their lifecycle in public and private markets. The Firm’s mission is to generate world-class returns for its investors and to do so in a way that makes its employees, partners and portfolio companies proud.”

## 2\. [Insight Partners](https://www.insightpartners.com/)

Insight Partners has focused on software since the firm’s inception in 1995. The firm has over [$80 billion in AUM](https://www.insightpartners.com/about-us/#:~:text=Insight%20Partners%20has%20invested%20in,%2C%20acquisition%20targets%2C%20and%20counsel.), investing in high-growth tech, software, and internet startups across every venture stage. Insight Partners has a track record of [55+ portfolio company IPOs](https://www.insightpartners.com/about-us/#:~:text=Insight%20Partners%20has%20invested%20in,%2C%20acquisition%20targets%2C%20and%20counsel.), and the firm has exited prominent SaaS names like DocuSign and Shopify. Their [typical first round](https://dealroom.co/guides/saas) is Series B.

When it comes to their experience in software, they [say](https://www.insightpartners.com/about-us/),

> “​​In 1995, we bet on our hunch that software would change the game. We’ve since partnered with hundreds of executives who’ve transformed and shaped industries, changing the way people live and do business. Insight’s approach is straightforward and results-driven. We deploy our three core pillars – scale, focus, and experience – to help companies capture value and generate long-term business success.”

## 3\. [Sequoia Capital](https://www.sequoiacap.com/)

Sequoia Capital has a portfolio of [more than 2,000 companies](https://www.crunchbase.com/organization/sequoia-capital) and has invested in top-notch SaaS companies such as Dropbox, Loom, Notion, HubSpot, Zoom, and Drift. Headquartered in Menlo Park, California, the firm is renowned for investing in innovative startups in various sectors, including finance, energy, healthcare, deep tech, and the internet. Their [typical first round](https://dealroom.co/guides/saas) is Series A.

What types of founders is Sequoia on the lookout for? They [say](https://www.sequoiacap.com/our-ethos/),

> “The creative spirits. The underdogs. The resolute. The determined. The indefatigable. The outsiders. The defiant. The independent thinkers. The fighters and the true believers. These are the founders with whom we partner. They’re extremely rare. And we’re ecstatic when we find them.”

## 4\. [Accel](https://www.accel.com/)

Headquartered in Palo Alto, Accel Partners has a track record of stellar SaaS investments, backing companies such as DocuSign, Dropbox, and Slack. Known for their commitment to supporting teams at very early stages in their journeys, Accel has been the initiating or lead investor in [more than 70%](https://accel.com/noteworthy/what-comes-next) of its portfolio companies over the past decade. Their [typical first round](https://dealroom.co/guides/saas) is Series A.

> “Our primary objective is to be the very first investor in our companies, take active, constructive roles with founders, and work patiently side-by-side to build incredible businesses. No short cuts. No company is too early and no check is too small.”

## 5\. [Andreessen Horowitz (a16z)](https://a16z.com/)

Founded by Marc Andreessen and Ben Horowitz in 2009, Andreessen Horowitz provides startups with access to expertise in innovation, executive and technical talent acquisition, market intelligence, policy and regulatory affairs, business development, and marketing and brand-building. Andreessen Horowitz invests across a wide range of sectors including AI, bio + healthcare, consumer crypto, enterprise, and fintech. Their [typical first round](https://dealroom.co/guides/saas) is Series A.

With [$44 billion in committed capital](https://a16z.com/about/), the firm is stage-agnostic and has invested in SaaS companies like Asana, Coinbase, and GitHub. For SaaS enthusiasts, Andreessen Horowitz’s [blog](https://a16z.com/news-content/) is worth bookmarking—their deal partners frequently discuss the latest SaaS trends, as well as predictions for the future.

A core belief at Andreessen Horowitz revolves around respect for founders. They [say](https://a16z.com/about/),

> “Respect for the entrepreneur and the company-building process [defines a16z](https://a16z.com/about). We know what it’s like to be in the founder’s shoes. General partners lead the firm, many of whom are former founders/operators, CEOs, or CTOs of successful technology companies, and have domain expertise ranging from data to artificial intelligence, biology to crypto, distributed systems to security, and marketplaces to financial services.”

## 7\. [Antler](https://www.antler.co/)

Antler is a Singapore-based VC firm that has expanded its reach globally—from India and Brazil to Canada and the US, and into global hubs like London, New York, and Berlin. It invests primarily in early-stage startups around the world.

They are one of the most active early-stage investors, having invested in over 1,300 startups since their founding in 2018. Their [typical first round](https://dealroom.co/guides/saas) is seed-stage.

In 2023, Antler [launched](https://techcrunch.com/2023/06/07/antler-branches-out-from-pre-seed-to-later-stage-investing-with-285m-fund/) a $285 million fund for later-stage investing, enabling them to support companies from Europe to Silicon Valley. Magnus Grimeland, Antler’s founder and CEO, [explains](https://techcrunch.com/2023/06/07/antler-branches-out-from-pre-seed-to-later-stage-investing-with-285m-fund/) how the fund fits into the firm’s strategy:

> “What we realized is, to be a great partner to the best founders it’s important to be with them for the long term, right? If we can say to incredible founders when they’re coming in, that we will not only be there to back you in the first round, but we’re also there to back you in the second round, in the third round and the fourth round… that means a lot, knowing that your first investor is still on board.”

## What differentiates top venture capital firms

What helps top firms stay ahead? Our 2024 investment benchmark report researched [the activities of top venture capital firms](https://www.affinity.co/report/the-2024-investment-benchmark-report) that use Affinity and revealed three key characteristics of top VCs:

1. **Regular communication**: Leading VCs consistently generated more engagement, sending and receiving 35% more emails than other firms over the one-year period ending in Q1 2024.
2. **Network depth:** Top firms prioritized their _existing_ network, adding fewer new contacts to their networks, while other firms consistently grew their network throughout the year.
3. **Data-driven decisions:** Compared to their peers, leading VCs are more likely to analyze more data sources. In a survey for our 2025 predictions, 60% reported using more than [four data sources](https://www.affinity.co/report/affinity-investment-predictions-report) when evaluating deals.
